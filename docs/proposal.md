# BORROWED STONE

> Working title. Exploration-first browser slice implemented; expansion remains subject to stakeholder review.

## Elevator pitch

**The old world is gone. Its stones are still useful.**

A route-reader reconnects a settlement built from forgotten civilizations. Read
altered masonry, operate old infrastructure, and decide how a repair should serve
the people living there now. This is dark fantasy with wonder, melancholy, and
hope—not horror or a quest to restore an empire.

## Core pillars

- Curiosity opens routes; knowledge replaces damage statistics.
- History is physical evidence, with interpretations recorded separately.
- The present is creative and worthwhile, not a diminished ancient world.
- Weathered enamel and living cloth distinguish old authority from new habitation.
- Every major discovery grants understanding, access, or a visible consequence.

## Target audience & platform

Players who enjoy environmental storytelling and unhurried exploration. The slice
runs in a desktop browser with keyboard controls. No combat, countdowns, external
accounts, downloads of art/audio, or mandatory collection checklist.

## Player experience (first 10 minutes)

Arrive in Reedbank, meet its route steward, gardener, and ceramic repairer. Inspect
a shrine reused from a sluice housing at the washed crossing. Its maintenance
diagram leads through a counterweight and reflector to a release mechanism.
The old channel would restore water but destroy a living garden. Investigating a
later Concord bypass offers another route. Return to the steward to finish, then
continue exploring the changed settlement.

## Core loop

Observe → investigate → understand → operate → open a route → return with consequences.

## Scope & milestones

Implemented: Reedbank, the washed crossing, reservoir courtyard, buried service
passage; one puzzle chain; optional ownership seal and bypass; three residents;
two persistent outcomes; a sketchbook; title, ending, restart, local save;
large text, navigation assistance, reduced motion, and opt-in procedural sound.

Approval gates remain concept, representative visual scene, playable validation,
then expansion. The build is a compact slice rather than full production.
Combat, crafting, procedural worlds, extensive branching, touch controls, and a
large open world are out of scope.

## Risks

- Beautiful but passive scenery: validate that clues change access or decisions.
- Opaque clues: repeatable dialogue, objective text, and optional destination markers.
- Exposition overload: short observations, with deeper interpretation in the sketchbook.
- Local save loss: make device-only storage and failures visible; no cloud-save claim.

## Open questions for the stakeholder

1. Does wonder grounded in melancholy, with genuine hope, match the desired tone?
2. Should later chapters remain combat-free?
3. Are clear historical events with debatable motives the right balance?
4. How prominent should residents become alongside solitary exploration?
5. Does the enamel-and-cloth visual identity merit further bespoke art production?
6. Should consequences remain local or eventually influence regions?
7. What audience, accessibility requirements, production budget, and release
   expectations should guide any expansion beyond this browser slice?
