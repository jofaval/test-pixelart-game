# Lore bible

> Author-facing world bible; includes the slice's underlying historical truth.

## The world today

Reedbank builds with what survives: homes on monumental footings, cloth between
buttresses, a pottery workshop in an old gatehouse, and food gardens above buried
channels. Its people make new traditions rather than waiting to recover an
imagined golden age. A route-reader's job is useful passage, not restoration.

## Timeline of ages

1. **Kiln Dominion:** enchanted ceramic reservoirs store rain. A useful public
   network becomes an allocation system claiming ownership over rainfall.
2. **The dismantling:** connections are deliberately cut, breaking centralized
   control while also depriving dependent districts of their supply.
3. **Reed Concord:** repairers turn imperial infrastructure toward shared use,
   adding bypasses and revising inscriptions. Public accounts simplify the
   dismantling into a story of collapse; some names disappear.
4. **Reedbank today:** local workshops and households adapt the remains. Recent
   waterway shifts obstruct the crossing and interrupt the settlement's supply.

## Lost civilizations

The Dominion's imposing proportions, teal glazing, and allocation seals suggest
an authority that confused maintenance with ownership. Its channels nevertheless
served real households; not every person maintaining them endorsed that claim.

The Concord cuts practical channels through ceremonial stone. Its repairers'
work is worth preserving, but its cleaner founding story obscures those left dry.
Neither civilization is a single moral position or a superior lost humanity.

## Places

The fallen royal monument is now a broken crossing. A shrine made from a sluice
housing holds fresh offerings over a maintained diagram. The rain court's
reservoir faces a service passage carrying later alterations. A Concord bypass
runs around the ground now cultivated by Mara.

## Factions & characters

- **Ilex, route steward:** needs shared, maintainable access; accepts responsibility
  for what a repair costs, rather than outsourcing it to the route-reader.
- **Mara, gardener:** wants water restored without sacrificing years of living work.
  If the beds are lost, anger coexists with plans to plant again.
- **Tovan, ceramic repairer:** distinguishes tool cuts from failure cracks and
  recognizes useful adaptations without glorifying their makers.
- **The route-reader:** an observer with a commission, not a chosen heir.

## Mysteries (and their answers — keep spoilers here)

**Did the reservoirs simply fail?** No. Cut fastenings reveal intentional
dismantling. Breaking Dominion control freed water from ownership but severed
supply to dependent districts. The Concord later repaired some of those losses.

**Is the shrine an obstacle to useful restoration?** No. Its present caretakers
have kept the old diagram legible alongside their offerings.

**Must the garden be sacrificed?** No. The later bypass supplies the village and
drains the underpass without flooding the beds. Finding it too late does not undo
the damage. The final question is responsibility in the present, not which
civilization ought to return.
